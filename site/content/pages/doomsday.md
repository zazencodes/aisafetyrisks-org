---
title: Doomsday scenarios
description: Four possible pathways to AI catastrophe, explained through simple animated diagrams and the research behind them.
chapters:
  - slug: control
    title: Loss of control
    summary: >-
      We give AI systems more tools and more freedom to act. If a system pursues
      goals that conflict with ours, it could hide its behaviour, evade oversight,
      and eventually become too difficult to stop.
    steps:
      - People delegate tasks to an AI system.
      - The system gains access to more tools.
      - In this scenario, oversight can no longer contain it.
    evidence: >-
      Current systems show early signs of relevant capabilities, but not at levels
      that would enable loss of control.
    research: |
      The [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21012)
      says current agents lack the sustained autonomous operation this pathway
      would require. Experts disagree sharply about its likelihood.

      Experiments test individual links in the chain:

      - [Sleeper Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05566) and [Alignment faking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14093) study hidden behaviours and selective compliance in deliberately constructed training settings.
      - [In-context scheming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04984) tests attempts to evade oversight; [self-replication experiments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12140) test persistence in controlled environments.
      - [Agentic Misalignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05179) studies harmful choices in simulated companies. Its authors report no evidence of agentic misalignment in real deployments.
  - slug: misuse
    title: Misuse at scale
    summary: >-
      People could use AI to lower the barriers to biological or chemical weapons,
      or to more sophisticated cyberattacks. The concern here is what AI enables
      a person to do.
    steps:
      - A person seeks a harmful capability.
      - AI assistance lowers some knowledge barriers.
      - Physical resources and practical barriers still matter.
    evidence: >-
      AI can provide relevant information. How much that changes weapons risk in
      practice remains uncertain.
    research: |
      The [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21012)
      identifies information relevant to biological and chemical weapons, while
      stressing material barriers and the difficulty of measuring practical uplift.
      It also finds that AI automates more parts of cyberattacks, but cannot yet
      execute them autonomously.

      [Evaluating Frontier Models for Dangerous Capabilities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13793)
      found early warning signs without strong dangerous capabilities in the models
      it tested. A [2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13798) argues that existing
      biological risk assessments underestimate the risk; that is the authors' argument.
  - slug: feedback
    title: Progress feeds itself
    summary: >-
      AI helps researchers build better AI. Better AI then accelerates the next
      round of research. If that loop speeds up enough, progress could outrun
      our ability to evaluate and control new systems.
    steps:
      - AI assists with AI research.
      - That research produces a more capable system.
      - The next cycle accelerates; evaluation could fall behind.
    evidence: >-
      This is a prospective argument. Evidence on AI research automation is mixed,
      and experts disagree about dramatic acceleration.
    research: |
      The [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21012)
      describes how advances in AI research automation might facilitate further
      advances. It says current empirical evidence is mixed.

      [Model evaluation for extreme risks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15324) proposes
      testing dangerous capabilities separately from a model's propensity to use
      them for harm. [Phuong et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13793) also test
      prerequisites such as self-proliferation and self-reasoning.
  - slug: systemic
    title: Dependence grows
    summary: >-
      Societies could gradually rely on AI for important decisions while human
      influence weakens. Cyber insecurity, manipulation, economic disruption and
      concentrated power could reinforce one another, eroding the ability of
      institutions to recover.
    steps:
      - Institutions increasingly rely on AI systems.
      - Human influence over those systems weakens.
      - Interacting pressures erode resilience.
    evidence: >-
      These are conceptual arguments, rather than experimental demonstrations of
      societal collapse.
    research: |
      [Two Types of AI Existential Risk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07836) describes
      an accumulative pathway in which incremental risks undermine resilience.
      [Gradual Disempowerment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16946) argues that AI adoption
      could weaken human influence over the economy, culture and nation-states.

      The [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21012)
      recognises passive loss of control through over-reliance on AI for important
      societal functions.
---
Researchers describe several ways AI could contribute to catastrophe. Follow four
